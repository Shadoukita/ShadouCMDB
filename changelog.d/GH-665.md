### Security: connections that send no request, or take none of their response, no longer hold a place under the connection limit

`HTTP_HEADER_READ_TIMEOUT_SECS` applied only once a connection was known to be HTTP/1. A client
that connected and sent nothing, sent part of the HTTP/2 connection preface, or opened an HTTP/2
connection without a request was never disconnected, and each such connection kept its place under
the open connection limit. A client could also keep its place by asking for a response and never
taking it: an HTTP/1 client that stops reading the socket, or an HTTP/2 client that keeps its
flow-control window shut. A few client networks could take every place without signing in, and
the server then refused every other connection ([GH#665], [GH#682]).

A connection with no request in progress for `HTTP_HEADER_READ_TIMEOUT_SECS` (default 10 s),
counted from when it was accepted or from when its last request started or ended, is now closed
whatever its protocol; an HTTP/2 client is sent a `GOAWAY` first. A connection whose client takes
none of a response for the same time is dropped. A response that is slow to come, or one the client
reads slowly, is not cut by these limits. HTTP/2 clients reconnect when they need to, as HTTP/1
clients already did after the same idle time. No configuration, API or schema change.

A client that takes a response a few bytes at a time can still hold its connection for as long as
the response lasts; a minimum transfer rate is not enforced.
