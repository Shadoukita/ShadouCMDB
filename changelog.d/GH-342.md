### Security: one client can no longer use up the budget for sign-in bodies still in transit

The routes that need no sign-in (setup, sign-in and its MFA step, OIDC sign-in) read their body
before they take a request slot, and the bytes of bodies still arriving count against a shared
budget of 64 KiB per `HTTP_MAX_CONCURRENT_REQUESTS`. Without a request buffering reverse proxy, one
client could spend that budget, so other users' sign-ins whose body arrived in more than one piece
got `503 SERVER_BUSY` ([GH#342]). The budget also had no upper limit, and a body that had sent
nothing yet did not count at all, so the number of such requests waiting at once was not limited
([GH#343]).

Now one client network (an IPv4 /24 or IPv6 /64, taken from `TRUSTED_PROXIES` as for the sign-in
throttle) holds at most a sixteenth of the budget, and the budget is capped at 256 MiB (at least
16 MiB; 32 MiB by default, unchanged). A body that has to wait for more data also counts 4 KiB
for its connection, so a client network can keep at most about 500 sign-in bodies waiting at the
defaults, and the server about 8,000. Requests past these limits get `503 SERVER_BUSY` with
`Retry-After` at once. A sign-in whose body arrives in one piece, as from browsers and most
clients, never counts and is not affected. An HTTP/2 connection now carries at most 100 concurrent
requests (200 before). No configuration, API or schema change.

**Upgrade:** nothing to do. Behind a reverse proxy that does not buffer request bodies and is not
listed in `TRUSTED_PROXIES`, every client shares the proxy's network and therefore one sixteenth of
the budget; list the proxy in `TRUSTED_PROXIES`, or let it buffer request bodies, as the
[hardening checklist][hardening-proxy] recommends.
