### Security: A requester can no longer choose the approver of a step whose approvers are business service owners (GH#708, GH#709)

The GH#664 fix stopped a requester from choosing their approver by editing the CI field a step
takes its approvers from. Two paths around it remained:

- **Business service owners (GH#708).** A step can take its approvers from the technical or
  business owners of the business services the CI belongs to (approver source `service_owner`). A
  requester with the edit right on business services could make a colleague an owner of a service
  the CI is in, or add the CI to a colleague's service, and then request. Now, when a request is
  made, an owner is not used if the requester made them an owner (the latest owner change that
  added them was theirs), and none of a service's owners are used if the requester added the CI to
  that service. The rules for fields apply here too: changes through an API token or bulk import
  count, a change through a token or import that recorded no user is not trusted, and an owner or
  membership with no audit history is kept.
- **Edits through a lent API token (GH#709).** An edit made with an API token used to count only
  as the token owner's. It now also counts as the edit of the user who minted the token for that
  owner. A requester who mints a token for a colleague and changes the approver field or the
  service owners with it no longer picks the approver. The token is found through the request's
  `token.use` audit row. If that row was pruned (`prune-audit --scope auth`), every user who
  minted one of the owner's tokens before the edit counts.
- Owners and memberships not used appear in the request's `droppedSources` like a dropped field
  (reason `field_set_by_requester` or `field_set_by_unattributed`; the label names the owner or
  the membership and the service). `fieldLastChanged` gains `tokenCreatedBy` for a change made
  through a token someone else minted. The approver preview with `ciId` and `requestedBy` lists
  them in the new `droppedParts` of a service owner source.

Upgrade: no migration. Requests already pending keep their approvers until an administrator's
**Refresh**, which applies the new rules.

Remaining limits: adding a colleague to a user group that owns a service is not covered (it needs
the user management right); someone other than the requester can still make the change on their
behalf (the audit log shows who did); changes older than the audit retention period are not seen.
