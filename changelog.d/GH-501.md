### Security: request slots and the sign-in body budget are shared out more fairly

Three limits on requests in progress were too loose:

- A sign-in body still arriving counted 4 KiB for its connection, but holds about 66 kB of memory.
  Slow senders could keep about 8,000 such bodies waiting and grow the server by about 500 MB
  ([GH#501]). Each now counts 64 KiB, so at most 512 wait at the defaults (4,096 at the 256 MiB
  ceiling of the budget).
- One signed-in user, or an API token, could take every `HTTP_MAX_CONCURRENT_REQUESTS` slot by
  sending request bodies slowly, so every other user got `503 SERVER_BUSY` until
  `HTTP_REQUEST_TIMEOUT_SECS` passed ([GH#502]). One user, with all their sessions and API tokens
  together, now holds at most a quarter of the slots (at least one).
- One client network (an IPv4 /24 or IPv6 /64) holds at most a sixteenth of the sign-in body
  budget, but a client with an IPv6 /56 holds 256 of them and could fill the budget alone
  ([GH#504]). One wider network (an IPv4 /16 or IPv6 /48) now holds at most a quarter of it.

Requests past these limits get `503 SERVER_BUSY` with `Retry-After` at once. A sign-in whose body
arrives in one piece, as from browsers and most clients, never counts against the body budget and
is not affected. No configuration, API or schema change.

**Upgrade:** nothing to do. A script that runs more than a quarter of `HTTP_MAX_CONCURRENT_REQUESTS`
requests at once as one user (128 at the default of 512) now gets `503 SERVER_BUSY` for the extra
ones; lower its concurrency, or raise `HTTP_MAX_CONCURRENT_REQUESTS`.
