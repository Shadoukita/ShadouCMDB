### Added: Approval policies and approvers on workflow transitions (design time)

- A transition in a workflow draft can carry an approval policy (`approval`). The policy has 1 to 5
  steps, run in order. Each step has a key, a name, the number of approvals it needs (1 to 20), an
  optional due interval (`dueAfter`, an ISO 8601 duration from `PT15M` to `P90D`), what happens when
  the step is overdue (`flag` or `reject`), and separation-of-duties flags (`distinctFromEarlier`,
  `excludeActorsOf`, `allowApiTokens`). The policy is part of the published version and its
  checksum. A version without a policy keeps the checksum it had before this release, so existing
  versions and configuration files compare as before.
- New endpoints `GET` and `PUT /api/v1/admin/workflow-definitions/{id}/approvers` set who may
  decide each step: a permission profile, a user group, a named user, a reference field of the CI
  that points at the Person type, or the technical or business owners of the business services the
  CI is a direct member of. An assignment can also be an `escalation` approver, who may decide only
  once the step is overdue. Changes are audited by name on the workflow definition. They need
  `workflows.manage`.
- `GET /api/v1/admin/workflow-definitions/{id}/approvers/preview` shows who could decide a step,
  for one CI or in general. For each user it says whether they are eligible, and if not, why:
  disabled account, no view right on the CI's type, the requester (four-eyes), or escalation only.
- Publishing and the approvers endpoints lint the policy. A step that excludes the actors of a
  transition the version does not have is an error. These are warnings: a step with no approver,
  approvers who cannot view the type, fewer approvers than the step needs, and an assignment for a
  step the version does not have.
- A reference field that names approvers cannot be archived, retyped or purged (409 `IN_USE`,
  naming the workflow and step).
- Configuration files are now format version 9. A workflow carries its approval policies in its
  graph and its approvers in `approvers`, with groups and users by name. Groups and users are not
  part of a configuration file. If a file names a group or user that does not exist on the target,
  the import fails and nothing is imported. Files of format versions 1 to 8 still import.
- Running a transition that has an approval policy is refused with 409 `CONFLICT`
  (`approval_required`) until a later release adds approval requests. Such a transition never runs
  without approval.

**Upgrade:** no migration and no action. Nothing changes for an install until an administrator
publishes a workflow version with an approval policy. Servers before this release cannot read
format 9 configuration files.
